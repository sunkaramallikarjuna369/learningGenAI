The art of communicating with AI

Step 1: Good vs Bad Prompts

What's Happening?

The same AI can give very different results based on how you ask. Vague prompts get vague answers. Specific prompts get specific answers. Compare the prompts below to see the difference!

Technical Details

Prompt quality affects the probability distribution over outputs. Specific prompts constrain the output space, reducing entropy and increasing the likelihood of desired responses. This is why prompt engineering is crucial for production systems.

Step 3: Advanced Techniques

What's Happening?

Different techniques work for different tasks. Zero-shot is direct asking, few-shot shows examples, and chain-of-thought asks for step-by-step reasoning. Try each technique below!

Technical Details

Zero-shot relies on pre-trained knowledge. Few-shot leverages in-context learning to adapt to new patterns. Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting improves reasoning by decomposing problems, especially effective for math and logic tasks.

Zero-Shot Prompting

Ask directly without examples. Works well for simple, common tasks.

Prompt:
Classify the sentiment of this review as positive, negative, or neutral: "The food was amazing but the service was slow."
Response:
Mixed/Neutral - The review contains both positive (amazing food) and negative (slow service) elements.

Few-Shot Prompting

Provide examples to guide the model. Great for specific formats or edge cases.

Prompt:
Classify sentiment:
"I love this!" → positive
"Terrible experience" → negative
"It's okay" → neutral
"The food was amazing but the service was slow." →
Response:
mixed

Chain-of-Thought Prompting

Ask for step-by-step reasoning. Essential for math, logic, and complex problems.

Prompt:
A store sells apples for $2 each. If you buy 5 or more, you get 20% off. How much do 7 apples cost? Think step by step.
Response:
Step 1: Calculate regular price = 7 × $2 = $14
Step 2: Check discount eligibility: 7 ≥ 5, so discount applies
Step 3: Calculate discount = $14 × 20% = $2.80
Step 4: Final price = $14 - $2.80 = $11.20
Answer: $11.20
