Step 1: Core Principles

What's Happening?

Responsible AI is built on key principles that ensure AI systems are fair, transparent, and beneficial. These principles guide how we design, build, and deploy AI systems.

Technical Details

Responsible AI frameworks include fairness metrics (demographic parity, equalized odds), explainability methods (LIME, SHAP), privacy techniques (differential privacy, federated learning), and governance structures (model cards, audit trails).

Step 2: Detecting Bias

What's Happening?

AI can inherit biases from training data, leading to unfair treatment of certain groups. We need to measure and detect these biases to fix them. Watch how different groups might receive different outcomes!

Technical Details

Fairness metrics include: Demographic Parity (equal positive rates across groups), Equalized Odds (equal TPR and FPR), Calibration (predictions match actual probabilities). Use tools like Fairlearn, AIF360, or What-If Tool.

Step 3: Explainability

What's Happening?

People deserve to understand why AI made a decision about them. Explainability shows which factors influenced the AI's decision, making it transparent and trustworthy.

Technical Details

Explainability methods: LIME (local linear approximations), SHAP (Shapley values for feature attribution), attention visualization, counterfactual explanations ("what would change the decision?").

Step 4: Ethical Scenarios

What's Happening?

Real-world AI applications face complex ethical dilemmas. Understanding these scenarios helps us make better decisions about when and how to use AI.

Technical Details

Ethical frameworks include consequentialism (outcomes), deontology (rules), virtue ethics (character). AI ethics requires balancing multiple stakeholders, considering long-term impacts, and establishing governance structures.
